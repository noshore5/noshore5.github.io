Chasing a 0.80 → 0.71 accuracy regression back to a fixed receptive field

2026-08-06 · EEG seizure prediction, dense-edge GNN

This one started as a visualization bug hunt and ended up explaining a real, if unglamorous, modeling gotcha.

Finding the real bug

Debug plots were built of the raw time series, their wavelet transforms, and the resulting coherence array for a single electrode pair. The first version was nearly all bright yellow — coherence values of 0.8–1.0 almost everywhere — which is not what a sparse, discriminative coherence map should look like. Tracing it back, the cause was downsampling: the wavelet coefficients were being resampled from about 1000 points down to 200 before coherence was computed, which smeared out real structure and produced spuriously high coherence even in frequency bands with essentially zero power in the raw transforms.

Coherence maps (channel pair × frequency) across ten trial samples, at native time resolution.

Rebuilding the coherence computation at native resolution — no resampling — produced a visibly correct, sparse map. Cone-of-influence masking was also added, so coherence values outside each wavelet's valid time-frequency support get excluded rather than contributing spurious high-coherence edges. Once the event extraction looked right, it was plugged back into the real training pipeline.

The regression

After combining native-resolution coherence, the COI mask, and a caching optimization for the wavelet transforms, subject-1 accuracy dropped from a remembered ~0.80 to ~0.76, and further runs fluctuated down toward 0.71–0.72. None of the obvious suspects moved the needle: coherence/phase thresholds, the COI mask itself, the smoothing kernel size, the frequency range, raw event density — every variant landed in a tight band around 0.76.

The actual cause was in the channel signal encoder: two stacked convolutional layers gave a fixed 17-sample receptive field regardless of sampling rate — about 68ms at the native 250Hz rate, which is shorter than a single cycle of mu-band activity (8–12Hz, roughly 83–125ms). The encoder simply couldn't see a full oscillation.

The twist: the old, "good" 0.80 pipeline had a resampling bug that happened to stretch that same 17-sample window over a ~5× larger real-time span. The old score wasn't better because the model was better — it was inadvertently getting a wider effective receptive field as a side effect of the same bug that was corrupting the coherence array.

The fix was a dilation parameter on the channel encoder (dilation=5, giving a receptive field of 81 samples — about 324ms, roughly 3.2 mu-band cycles), which widens the encoder's real-time window without resampling or discarding any signal, and without adding parameters. That recovered accuracy to parity (~0.80 on subject 1) while keeping the coherence computation, the COI mask, and the raw signal all at correct native resolution.

The honest caveat

Going native-resolution did not improve accuracy beyond the old, buggy pipeline's ceiling — it only recovered parity. A much larger smoothing kernel was re-tested afterward, giving 0.8008 versus 0.7991, still within noise. For this architecture and task, the correctness fixes were a wash on accuracy: real bugs, genuinely fixed, at zero accuracy cost — but not, on their own, a source of extra performance. Worth doing for correctness, not a result to oversell.
