Coherence is the signal, and the 0.674 leader holds up under audit

2026-08-28 – 2026-09-02 · ablations, reproduction, a real memory-leak fix

With hermitian_ssm spending a week trying to beat the temporal_graph_mamba leader outright, this thread asked a more skeptical pair of questions about the leader itself: is 0.674 actually real, and is the coherence graph doing anything a simpler representation couldn't? Both got clean, direct answers — and along the way, a genuine memory-leak bug turned out to be responsible for most of the "this run behaves strangely" reports of the last two weeks.

Capacity levers on the leader: also negative

Before questioning the representation, we tried the same "give it more to work with" playbook that failed on hermitian_ssm, directly on the leader itself.

The "post" result is worth a specific callout, because a partial run of it looked like a clean regression mid-flight — one fold came in at 0.41 against a baseline of 0.79 — and got written up that way before the other five folds finished. The completed six-fold run showed that was just that one fold's normal variance, not a real capacity failure. The lesson stuck: on this dataset, a killed-early partial result is not evidence, it's noise, and every negative verdict below was paid for with a full six-fold run rather than an early kill.

The null-baseline test: strip out coherence entirely

The cleanest possible test of "does the coherence graph matter" is to remove it and change nothing else. channel_cwt is exactly that ablation: per-channel wavelet power, no cross-channel information at all, run through the identical temporal block, same hyperparameters, same infrastructure — the only variable is coherence vs. no coherence.

Two folds in: AP 0.149 and 0.263, against the leader's 0.79 and 0.83 on the same folds. A roughly 0.6 AP collapse, with training curves that looked perfectly healthy on their own validation split — the same "trains fine, doesn't transfer" signature seen throughout this project, just far more severe. Killed after two folds; the gap was already conclusive.

A follow-up added one round of learned message-passing across all 23×22 channel pairs on top of the same per-channel features — testing whether it's cross-channel information in general that's missing, or the coherence cross-spectrum specifically. Result: dead flat around AP 0.17 on every fold, no better than the no-message-passing version. It's the phase relationship between channels — not just "knowing about other channels" — that the model actually needs.

Is 0.674 itself real?

The historical 0.674 number was days old, ran on CPU, and had been stitched together across three separate overnight process restarts — and a much earlier note had already flagged one fold's AP swinging between 0.29 and 1.00 on config that hadn't changed at all. Before trusting the null-baseline comparison above, we needed a clean, single, same-machine run to compare against.

A same-machine reproduction (this time on the Mac's GPU) came back at mean AP 0.644, with ROC-AUC actually higher than the historical run (0.973 vs. 0.94). The entire 0.03 gap traced to one fold's normal run-to-run variance; every other fold matched or beat the historical number. 0.674 is real — not a fluke, not an artifact of the earlier stitched-together run — and the null-baseline collapse above is measured against a properly validated baseline, not a lucky one.

A real bug, hiding in plain sight

While chasing why later folds in these six-fold runs kept getting slower and less stable, we found an actual memory-leak bug: the training loop never released a fold's model, optimizer, and per-batch tensors before starting the next fold, so reference cycles kept everything reachable until the garbage collector happened to run on its own. One affected run climbed to 25 GB of resident memory on a 16 GB machine by fold five — almost entirely swapped — and individual folds went from ~50 seconds an epoch to over an hour.

This had been quietly blamed on cache size and disk I/O in several earlier notes. It wasn't (or wasn't only) that — it was Python simply never letting go of the previous fold. An explicit teardown at the end of each fold fixed it: every fold now runs at a flat ~50–56 seconds regardless of position in the loop.

Pinning down the honest error bar

With the leak fixed and a faithful half-size cache format verified bit-for-bit against full precision, the last piece was measuring how much the result actually moves with nothing but random seed changed. A multi-seed, multi-epoch-count sweep (with validation held out, and separately with no validation split at all) converged on the same conclusion from two directions: the six-fold mean sits in a ~0.55–0.65 band with about ±0.06 seed-to-seed spread, almost all of it concentrated in two chronically unstable folds. A 20%-held-out validation split, with its implicit early-stopping, turns out to reach the same operating point as carefully tuning the epoch count by hand — so it stays in the default configuration rather than being dropped for a marginal training speedup.

Put together: the leader's number is real, coherence specifically (not just cross-channel information) is what's carrying it, and its honest uncertainty is now measured rather than assumed. That's three separate audits of the same claim, all converging — and a genuine bug found and fixed as a side effect of taking the audit seriously enough to run it to completion every time.
