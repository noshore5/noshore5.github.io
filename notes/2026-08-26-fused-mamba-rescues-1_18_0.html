Fused Mamba rescues the one bad fold, and CG-MambaNet hits a real mambapy wall

2026-08-26 · architecture ablation, infrastructure, seizure prediction

Yesterday's GRU-vs-Mamba comparison ended on an open question: Mamba's average precision beat GRU broadly, but its recall and F1 lagged almost entirely because of one seizure, 1_18_0, where recall collapsed to 0.100 despite a perfectly reasonable ranking score (AP 0.618). That looked like a calibration problem specific to one fold rather than a real weakness, but it was untested. This session tested it directly — and separately, hit a hard scaling wall trying to reproduce a published architecture locally.

Swapping the Mamba implementation, not the model

The original run used mambapy, a pure-PyTorch Mamba scan, at the repo's normal precision. A second run repeated the exact same six-fold protocol — same seizures, same channel subset, same epoch budget — but swapped in the mamba-ssm fused CUDA kernel with bf16 mixed precision for both the dense-edge stage and training. Two things changed at once (kernel and precision), so this isn't a clean ablation of either one alone — but it's a fast way to check whether the 1_18_0 failure was configuration-sensitive at all.

It was.

Five of six folds move by less than 0.06 F1 in either direction — noise-scale. The sixth, 1_18_0, jumps from Mamba's worst fold to its best: recall 0.100→0.633, precision up to 0.792, F1 0.171→0.704, false-alarm rate falling to zero after smoothing. For comparison, GRU's own F1 on that fold was 0.754 — fused Mamba lands in the same regime, closing almost the entire gap that made GRU look categorically better on recall.

The other fold that completed this session, 1_26_0, stayed nearly identical to the original (AP 0.229→0.231, F1 0.364→0.387) — useful as a negative control. If the fused configuration were just generically better across the board, you'd expect every fold to move. Instead the change is concentrated almost entirely on the one fold that was failing, which is the signature of a fix, not a general improvement.

Across the completed six folds, fused Mamba now leads GRU, the original Mamba, DBConformer, and SlimSeiz on the project's primary metric:

The honest caveat: kernel and precision changed together, so this doesn't prove which one did the rescuing, or whether it's the interaction of both. The next controlled step is separating them — fused kernel at full precision, or mambapy at bf16 — but the headline claim from yesterday's note (that this looked like a threshold/calibration artifact on one fold, not a real architectural weakness) held up.

A second thread: CG-MambaNet, built but stuck on CPU

Separately, this session built CG-MambaNet — a CNN–GCN–Mamba–BiLSTM architecture from a recent preprint reporting 0.8152 cross-patient AUC on CHB-MIT, with no public code to work from. The build came from the paper's own methods section (unusually detailed for a preprint) wired into this repo's existing leave-one-seizure-out protocol for a fast, same-protocol read against the existing backbones — not an attempt to reproduce the paper's own multi-patient number, which would need montage unification across subjects that doesn't exist here yet.

The architecture wired up cleanly and passed a CPU smoke test end-to-end. Scaling it to the paper's real hyperparameters did not go as smoothly. A first sizing bug (reading the paper's embedding width as the raw per-patch sample count, giving d_model=256 instead of a sane 64) was found and fixed — a 21× speedup on its own. What was left after that fix wasn't fixable by tuning: a 12-layer, bidirectional Mamba encoder at the paper's real sequence length showed severely super-linear backward-pass scaling under mambapy's pure-PyTorch scan — on CPU, doubling batch size roughly quadrupled runtime; on MPS it was worse (17.8× for a 2× batch increase, then an outright OOM at batch 16). Switching to mambapy's sequential-scan fallback didn't help either — forward stayed cheap, but backward exploded to nearly 16 minutes at batch 16, an autograd graph roughly 24 Mamba instances × 480 timesteps deep.

Neither of mambapy's two scan modes is viable for this architecture's real depth and sequence length, on CPU or MPS. That's a library-level limitation at this scale, not a hyperparameter to tune around — and it's the same fused-kernel-vs-pure-PyTorch gap that's now shown up twice in two days. Given the choice between shrinking the architecture (a real deviation from the paper) or waiting for CUDA infrastructure, the call was to stop here rather than compromise it — resume on the fused-kernel image once it's available for this workload.

Also this session

The GitHub-release mirror for CHB-MIT (built for chb01 specifically to dodge PhysioNet's S3 throttling) was generalized to any subject, and chb02–chb04 were published to it — chb04 needed a two-part split to fit under GitHub's 2 GiB per-asset cap. Extending the matched GRU baseline to chb02 and chb03 surfaced two open threads: chb02 collapses to chance on every fold (likely too few preictal windows per LOSO split to learn from, not yet confirmed against other backbones), and chb03 shows a striking split where its earliest four seizures fail near-chance while its last three train well — flagged, not yet explained.

Open items

Kernel and precision still need separating before the 1_18_0 rescue is attributed to one or the other. chb02's total collapse and chb03's early/late split haven't been checked against dense_edge or dense_edge_mamba to see whether they're GRU-specific or a genuine data-difficulty signal. CG-MambaNet is parked at its paper-faithful hyperparameters until the fused-kernel CUDA image is available for it. And a chb02 seizure-ID off-by-one bug (labels shifted by one file number, same class of bug as an earlier chb01 artifact) was found but not yet fixed.
