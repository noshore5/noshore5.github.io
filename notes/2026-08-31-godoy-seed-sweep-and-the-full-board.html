A five-seed sweep turns a "near-tie" into a clear third place — and the full board, refreshed

2026-08-31 · godoy_tmc, seed variance, model comparison

A single run of the raw-signal Transformer baseline (godoy_tmc, a reconstruction of Godoy et al.'s TMC-Transformer) landed at mean AP 0.619 — close enough to the coherence-graph leader's same-machine number (0.644) to read as a near-tie for second place. One run is one seed, though, and this dataset's per-fold spread had already shown up everywhere else in this project. Five seeds later, the near-tie is gone.

Mean across seeds: 0.556, sample std 0.056. Seed 42 — the one originally reported — sat at the very top of the range, not the center. Nearly all of the spread traces to two folds: one ranges 0.11–0.55 across seeds, the other 0.37–0.83. The other four folds are stable (one is perfect on every seed). That's the exact same "two hard folds carry all the variance" structure the coherence-graph leader shows on its own seed sweep — but where the leader's range is 0.64–0.67, this model's is 0.47–0.62, nearly twice as wide relative to its mean.

The honest number for this baseline is 0.556 ± 0.056, not 0.619. That puts it a clear ~0.09 AP behind the coherence-graph leader — a real gap, not a coin flip — and it's worth saying plainly that this is also an off-label comparison: the architecture is a detection model from its original paper, reconstructed (not the authors' code), and run here in a prediction setting it wasn't designed for.

A follow-up capacity sweep (model width 16 vs. 32 vs. 64, one seed each) found an inverted U with the peak exactly at the paper's own stated width of 32 — both narrower and wider configurations lost about 0.10 AP, the wider one for the now-familiar reason: it fit its own validation split better and generalized worse to the held-out seizure. The architecture is already at its best known configuration; there's no width left to find.

Two structural notes on the board. First, the split down the middle is real: every pipeline that consumes this repo's CWT + coherence-graph features outperforms every raw-EEG architecture at a comparable position on the list, echoing the same finding as the null-baseline ablation elsewhere this week — coherence carries real signal that end-to-end learning on raw channels hasn't recovered on its own here. Second, the two coherence-graph rows near the bottom (enc-free GRU/Mamba) are stale references from before "pre"/"post" existed, run on different hardware entirely — not current contenders, just historical markers of how far the same feature family has come.

A cross-cutting pattern also worth flagging again: fold 1_18_0 is hard for nearly every raw-EEG model at the fixed decision threshold, while fold 1_26_0 is hard for almost everyone regardless of input representation. Both keep showing up as the two folds carrying each pipeline's own seed-to-seed variance — a recurring structural weak point in this six-seizure, one-subject evaluation that no single architecture change here has resolved.
