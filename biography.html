About

I'm a mathematician and researcher specializing in time-frequency analysis and signal processing, currently leading a small independent research team building a graph neural network + GRU architecture for EEG-based epileptic seizure prediction, using wavelet coherence methods to model signal propagation across electrode channels.

I grew up in Asheville, North Carolina, and studied mathematics at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where I competed for the track and field team, performed with the Carolina Bluegrass Band, and did honors research in computational fluid dynamics, implementing numerical solvers for the Navier–Stokes equations.

I later completed a research master's degree in mathematics at the University of Galway in Ireland, where my thesis on wavelet-based time-frequency fingerprinting was published on arXiv. My work on real-time wavelet coherence architecture was published as a peer-reviewed paper at IEEE ISPA 2025 (see Publications).

Along the way I've worked as a computational modeler in the wind energy sector, applying finite element methods and electromagnetic modeling, and taught machine learning and Python, designing custom curricula with an emphasis on explainability and first-principles reasoning.
